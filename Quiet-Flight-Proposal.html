01

What we found

You asked me to look at one glitch. On 15 September I pulled all the product data out of your store. The glitch is not the real problem. It is a sign of a bigger one. Here are three things, in the order they cost you money.

Sixteen board pages can be checked out for $0.00

It is not just The Marlin. Every model page has the same problem: Classic Funshape, Black Marlin, Ultra Glide, GTO Longboard, Jumbo Sting Fish, The Pontoon, The Skiff, Double Agent, Secret Agent, The Sea Cow, The Anti-Hero, The Reaper, The Vandal, The Bad Fish, The Mahi Mahi and The Marlin.

These pages were meant to be spec sheets, not products. But each one was saved in Shopify as a product. The price is zero and stock tracking is off. That means they never sell out, and anyone can buy them. Your website code is fine. The product records are wrong.

The Marlin, product record, pulled 15 Sep 2026price "0.00" inventory_management null (never runs out) requires_shipping false (no address collected) taxable false

This is also why people can order in quantities you never put up for sale. With stock tracking off, nothing stops someone putting fifty of a board in the cart. Shipping is switched off on those records too, so a $0.00 order does not even ask for an address. You first mentioned this in April last year, so it is worth checking your order history for any that went through.

All 31 boards in stock can be sold more than once

Every board you sell, new and used, has stock tracking turned off. Each one is a single board. So four people can buy the same 5'10 Reaper on the same afternoon, and you would only find out when you went to pack them. None of the boards have a SKU or a shipping weight either. Shopify thinks a surfboard weighs nothing, so it quotes the wrong postage.

Three separate listings exist for a 6'0 Anti Hero and two for a 6'0 Pontoon, under slightly different spellings.

Google no longer sends your brand name to you

Search "Quiet Flight" today. What comes back is surf shop listings, store directories and retail pages. That is because the retail chain was sold to Billabong in 2008, and it now belongs to Authentic Brands Group. Your own factory site sits near the bottom of a page you should own.

Now search "custom surfboards Cocoa Beach". That page belongs to Neilson, Oceansports World and Allen White. Tom Neilson is named on your own About page as one of the shapers who came through this factory. Today he ranks above you. He shows a price list starting at $1450 and lists the boards he has in stock. You show $0.00.

One smaller thing. You said to leave the About page as it is, and I will. But it still carries the theme's demo text at the bottom, under a heading called Our Services. It is three paragraphs about building relationships with partners in the public and private sectors. Your history stays exactly as written. Only the filler comes off.

04.1

What the four paid phases actually do

Section 02 lists what gets built. This section walks through it step by step, in the order the work happens. It also says what each phase changes for your business. If you read one part of this proposal, read this one.

Phase 2

The catalog, separate from the store

Weeks 1 to 3 $650

Right now your catalog and your shop are the same thing, which is why a spec sheet can be put in a cart. Pulling them apart is what you asked for, and it fixes the glitch as a side effect.

  1. Count the floor

    We get on a call and you walk the shop while we count what is there. Every board gets a stock number, a SKU and a real shipping weight. Right now not one of your 31 boards has any of those three.

  2. Turn the model pages into catalog pages

    The Marlin, the Reaper, the Pontoon and the other thirteen stop being products with a price of zero. They become pages you read, with no cart on them at all. This is how Lost does it, and it is the structure you pointed me to.

  3. Give each model its tabs

    Description, Dimensions, Photos, Video. The size run becomes a proper table with length, width, thickness and volume, so a rider can match a board to their weight without asking you.

  4. Load your photos and video

    Each model gets its own set, from what you send me. Video sits on Shopify rather than YouTube, so it loads fast and carries no outside branding.

  5. Add the new models

    Send me the list with dimensions and photos and they go in alongside the sixteen that are there.

  6. Connect each model to what is in stock

    Your 5'10 Marlin, your 6'2 Marlin and the used 5'9 Marlin all sit under the Marlin page. Someone reading about the Marlin sees the three you have, with prices, and a way to order one custom.

What this changes for you
  • Nobody can order a board that is not for sale, because there is nothing on the page to order.
  • Someone who lands on the Marlin page can see the Marlins you have. Today there is no path from the spec sheet to a board for sale, so that visitor leaves.
  • Each model becomes a page Google can rank, with real dimensions and real photos on it rather than a price of zero.
  • Video of a board being ridden does more selling than any paragraph you could write about it.
Phase 3

The store, six sections

Weeks 3 to 5 $500

The catalog tells people about boards. The store sells them. Six sections, as you listed them.

  1. Build the six sections

    In-stock surfboards, used surfboards, custom boards, factory seconds, apparel, hats. Each with its own page, its own look and its own rules.

  2. Sort the boards into them

    New boards, used boards and factory seconds stop being mixed into one list. A buyer looking for a cheap first board and a buyer looking for a new performance shortboard stop getting in each other's way.

  3. Merge the duplicates and publish prices

    Three listings for a 6'0 Anti Hero become one. Every board gets a price, photos and its dimensions written the same way.

  4. Switch on the filters

    Length, volume, board type, condition and price. This is the single biggest thing that stops a surfer leaving a shop page.

  5. Set up apparel and hats

    The sections, the page templates and the size and colour options, all working, with one example product in each so your team can copy the pattern and add the rest.

What this changes for you
  • You stop selling the same board twice. With tracking off, four people can buy one 5'10 Reaper and you find out when you go to pack them.
  • A buyer can cut your boards down to the three that suit their weight and ability without phoning the shop. Most people will not phone. They will just go somewhere else.
  • Factory seconds get their own shelf instead of looking like a mistake in the new board list.
  • Apparel and hats are ready to sell the day you have stock to put in them.
Phase 4

Custom board ordering

Weeks 4 to 6 $350

A board built to order is the most valuable thing your factory makes. Right now there is no way to order one on the website.

  1. You give me the numbers

    Base price for each model, and what each option adds: construction, fin plug setup, fin system, resin tint, a name on the stringer. Only you can give me these. This phase cannot start without them.

  2. You give me the real lead time

    How many weeks from deposit to a finished board. The page will say this before checkout, not after.

  3. Build the picker

    Model first, then a size from that model's real run, then construction, fins and tail. There is a notes box for anything the customer wants to tell the shaper.

  4. Wire up live pricing

    The total changes as the customer picks options, so nobody gets a surprise at the end.

  5. Make the order print for the bay

    Every spec comes attached to the order. It prints on one sheet that goes straight to the shaping bay.

  6. Run test orders before it goes live

    I place real test orders all the way through. I check the spec sheet comes out right before any customer sees it.

What this changes for you
  • You start taking custom orders at eleven at night. Tom Neilson shows a price list from $1450 and takes orders on his website. Right now anyone who wants to order outside your shop hours has to go to a competitor.
  • The phone call goes away. So do the mistakes that come from writing specs down during one.
  • Money arrives before foam is cut, if you choose the deposit option.
  • It builds on Phase 2. The sizes the picker offers come from the model pages, which is why the catalog has to be right first.
Phase 5

Modern updates and front page video

Weeks 5 to 6 $150

Small, on purpose. You said you are not looking to change the site much, so nothing here moves your layout or your theme.

  1. Add the video section to the front page

    Muted, looping, hosted on Shopify, with a still image behind it so the page never waits on it. About 10 to 20 seconds of the shaping floor.

  2. Tidy the type and spacing

    Sizes, spacing and buttons brought in line across the site. The pages keep their shape, they just stop looking uneven.

  3. Fix what Google reads first

    Your home page has no main heading at all. That, the page titles and the descriptions all get set properly.

  4. Speed pass

    Shrink the images, load them only when they are needed, and take out the scripts the site does not use. This matters more once there is video on the page.

What this changes for you
  • The first thing a visitor sees is a board being shaped in your factory. That is the one thing your competitors cannot copy.
  • The site feels current without you paying for a redesign you did not ask for.
  • Speed is what makes search and paid ads worth doing later. Adding video without a speed pass would make the site slower, not better.

The order matters. Phase 3 has nothing to sort until the catalog in Phase 2 is real, and Phase 4 takes its sizes straight from the model pages Phase 2 builds. Running them in this order is what keeps the total at $1,650 instead of paying twice for the same work.
